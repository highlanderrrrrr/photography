Hayward Jewels and Potential Jewels

Veterans Day in Hayward: a well-kept cemetery, the Japanese Gardens full of dads and little kids, and a quiet holiday downtown that's a little worn around the edges.

Some of you may have forgotten, but in the early 1900s Hayward was known as the Heart of the Garden of Eden, for its mild weather and rich soil: orchards, produce and flowers everywhere.

Today many of us mostly see the other side: the homelessness and the trash. But Hayward still has some very beautiful places, and this is the first of a series of outings to find them.

9:42 – 9:43 a.m.Holy Sepulchre Cemetery

I drove by the cemetery on Mission Boulevard and had to stop. As bleak as that sounds, Hayward has a really nice cemetery: green, quiet and well taken care of. It was Veterans Day.

10:13 – 11:17 a.m.Japanese Gardens and Downtown

Then the Hayward Japanese Gardens. It's free, anybody can walk in, and it's extremely clean and manicured, with benches and gazebos where you can sit, relax, think and ponder. A really unique place in such a dense city.

What got me were the fathers with their little kids, walking around, the kids running and climbing on the rocks. It made me miss my own kids at that age. We came here as a family many times when they were little; now they're teenagers, and there's no way they'd come with me.

From there I walked to Heritage Plaza, across C Street from the library. It's a park in the middle of the city, a bit run down, not the squeaky clean image, but a family was sitting there having lunch. The library was closed for the holiday, and downtown was quiet: not a great-looking city, not a bad one either, somewhere in the middle.

Hayward has so many jewels, and potential jewels, that feel a little neglected. I wish the city would give them the attention they deserve.
